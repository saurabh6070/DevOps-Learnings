# 🧑‍🏫 Teaching Playbook for AWS Classes

## 🎯 Purpose
This file is a reusable teaching template for presenting AWS topics clearly and practically in class.

## 🧭 Standard Teaching Flow
For every AWS topic, follow this pattern:

1. Start with a simple definition.
2. Explain why the service exists.
3. Show a real-world use case.
4. Draw a small architecture diagram.
5. Walk through a manual lab step by step.
6. Show the Terraform version for automation.
7. End with common interview questions and mistakes.

## 🧩 Example Teaching Template
### Step 1: Introduce the service
- What is it?
- Why do we use it?
- What problem does it solve?

### Step 2: Explain the architecture
- What are the key components?
- How do they connect?
- Where does it fit in a real application?

### Step 3: Demonstrate manually
- Create the resource in the AWS console.
- Configure the settings carefully.
- Verify the result.
- Show the student what success looks like.

### Step 4: Show Terraform
- Create the infrastructure as code.
- Explain the key blocks.
- Apply it and verify the resources.

### Step 5: Reinforce with examples
- Ask students to explain when they would use the service.
- Compare it with alternatives.
- Discuss trade-offs such as cost, scalability, and security.

## ✅ Teaching Tips
- Avoid jumping too quickly into advanced concepts.
- Let students repeat the steps themselves.
- Use the console first, then automation.
- Connect every service to a real scenario.
- Encourage questions during the lab.

## 📚 Suggested Topics to Teach in This Sequence
1. AWS introduction and global infrastructure
2. EC2 and Launch Templates
3. VPC, subnets, route tables, and NAT
4. S3 and storage classes
5. IAM and access control
6. KMS and encryption
7. RDS and DynamoDB
8. Load Balancing and Auto Scaling
9. CloudWatch and CloudTrail
10. SNS, SQS, and EventBridge
11. WAF and Shield
12. Route 53 and CloudFront
13. ECS, EKS, and Lambda
14. Terraform and CloudFormation

## 📝 Instructor Reminder
If a topic feels too long, break it into three parts:
- concept
- manual lab
- automation lab

That structure makes teaching much easier and more effective.
